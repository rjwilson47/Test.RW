About the Centre

The Hunter Wetlands Centre is a self-funded, community-owned, not-for-profit charity dedicated to conserving and celebrating the Hunter region's internationally significant wetlands.

Located on 45 hectares of rehabilitated land in Shortland, Newcastle, the site was once a dumping ground but has been transformed into a thriving conservation sanctuary.

Ramsar Significance

The Hunter Wetlands are recognised under the Ramsar Convention as wetlands of international significance. This designation highlights their importance for:

  • Supporting over 200 wildlife species
  • Providing critical habitat for vulnerable species including Magpie Goose and Freckled Duck
  • Acting as a strategic drought refuge for waterbirds
  • Supporting 28 breeding bird species on site
  • Serving as an important stopover for migratory shorebirds

Conservation

The centre works to regenerate and conserve local wildlife habitat through:

  • Habitat restoration and management
  • Monthly bird surveys and monitoring
  • Environmental education programs
  • School and community engagement
  • Native plant propagation

As a not-for-profit charity, your entry fee directly supports conservation efforts. Consider an annual membership ($25) to support ongoing work.

Habitats

Freshwater Ponds

Deep open water supporting waterfowl including Black Swan, Grey Teal and Hardhead.

Melaleuca Swamp

Freshwater swamp forest used as a heronry by 4 egret species and an evening roost for ibis.

Marshes & Mudflats

Seasonal habitat providing foraging areas for migratory shorebirds when water levels drop.

Woodland

Native woodland providing habitat for bush birds, mammals and reptiles.

Ironbark Creek

Tidal creek winding through the site, accessible by canoe, leading to rainforest.
